## How to use this inventory

- **Verified legacy content** means the item appeared on a first-party Falola Psychiatry Practice URL during the audit. It does not mean the underlying claim has been independently credentialed or approved for the new site.
- **External/unverified** means the item was found on a third-party service or in search results and must not be published without confirmation.
- **Missing** identifies content the new page needs but the legacy site does not supply.
- Legacy policy prose is summarized without changing its factual effect. The practice should supply a controlled, legally reviewed source document rather than treating a Wix page as the canonical policy.

## Patient Information (`/patient-information`)

Source: `https://www.falolapsychiatry.com/office-fees-policies`

### Check-in and payment facts shown

- Valid identification is required at each visit.
- Patients, parents, or guardians should report address, phone, and insurance changes.
- The page says insured patients owe the copay at service and may be asked for a `$100` deductible deposit, with overpayments refunded on request after an explanation of benefits or credited to the account.
- The page lists self-pay fees of `$300` for a new-patient consultation and `$175` for follow-up, says additional follow-up time may be allotted, and says brief psychotherapy is included in every appointment.
- Past-due balances and copays are due at service; returned checks carry a `$30` fee.
- Cash, check, debit cards, and most major credit cards are listed as accepted.

### Attendance facts shown

- The page asks for 72 hours' cancellation notice.
- Arriving more than 10 minutes late or not rescheduling more than one business day before the appointment is described as producing a no-show/cancellation fee equal to the appointment fee.

### Medication refill facts shown

- Stable chronic-condition refills may be requested by phone during business hours if the patient was seen within the prior three months.
- Patients are asked to request refills during visits and allow 48–72 hours for a response.
- The physician reserves discretion to refuse refills after missed or frequently canceled appointments; the page says this may also lead to dismissal.
- No after-hours or weekend refills are offered.

### Treatment and safety facts shown

- Nonurgent calls/messages are handled during business hours with up to two business days requested for response.
- Existing patients are directed to call or text `(659) 346 6314` for urgent needs and may use online scheduling for an urgent appointment.
- Safety concerns are directed to 911 or the nearest emergency department.

### Controlled-medication facts shown

- Controlled medications are issued for no more than three months at a time, and appointments must be kept for refills.
- Lost medication is not rewritten before the next refill time.
- Random urine drug screening may occur; failure to complete requested screening may lead to refused refills or dismissal.
- The page cites the Alabama Pain Management Act of 2013 for a three-month visit interval for Schedule II narcotics.
- New patients are queried in a controlled-substance database; results involving pain-clinic dismissal or multiple narcotic prescribers may cause the physician, at their discretion, to decline or withdraw care.

### Laboratory facts shown

- Requested laboratory work or urine drug screens are presented as part of treatment; outside facilities may be needed.
- Refills or further prescribing may be withheld until requested tests are completed.
- New patients and patients receiving controlled medication may need an initial urine drug screen; uncovered charges are the patient's responsibility.
- The page states urine drug screens are required every three to six months and reserves random screens and pill counts.

### Documents and records facts shown

- Physician-signature forms may have a fee.
- Records may be faxed to another physician without charge.
- Other paper copies may carry a legally permitted copying fee.
- School/work absence documentation is issued only when the patient was seen.

### Dismissal facts shown

- Dismissed patients may no longer schedule, obtain refills, or treat the practice as their physician.
- Medication transition is described as at physician discretion, with a minimum of 30 days.
- Examples include missed appointments, verbal/physical abuse toward staff, and unpaid bills.
- Notice is sent to the listed address.

### Privacy facts shown

- The electronic chart may be accessed by covering clinicians and limited trained staff for scheduling/refills.
- The page says outside release requires written consent or a court order, then lists exceptions involving imminent danger, suspected child/elder abuse or neglect, court orders, malpractice or billing disputes, insurer review, credit-card statements, collections (including diagnostic/procedure codes), staff, and professional consultation.
- Patients may complete a release authorizing discussion of care or account matters.
- The page states that children under 17 have a right to confidential exchanges with clinicians.

### Missing or unresolved

- A dated, version-controlled policy approved by counsel/compliance.
- Formal Notice of Privacy Practices and acknowledgement process.
- Exact business hours, refill contact channel, and emergency language.
- Governing legal citations; the page's `CMS.org` reference is ambiguous.
- Resolution of fee conflicts documented under Appointments.
- Review of minor-consent/confidentiality, controlled-medication, testing, dismissal, collection, and records language.
